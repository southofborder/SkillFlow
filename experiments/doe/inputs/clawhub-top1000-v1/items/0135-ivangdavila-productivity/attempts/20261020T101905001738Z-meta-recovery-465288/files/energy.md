# Energy — The Capacity Everything Else Spends

Scope: sleep, recovery, breaks, and matching work to the body's actual state. Energy is not a motivational topic here: it is the term `focus_hours_target` stands for, and no scheduling technique adds hours to a depleted system.

**Before giving energy advice**, read `## Energy Patterns`, `## Constraints` and `## Friction` in `~/Clawic/data/productivity/memory.md`, plus the shared `~/Clawic/data/health/profile.md` if it exists — a stated condition or medication timing changes every recommendation below.

## Sleep Is the Base

- The AASM and its partner societies recommend **7 or more hours** a night for adults on a regular basis; below that, attention and error rates degrade before the person notices they have degraded. That last part is the operationally important one: self-rated performance recovers with caffeine long before actual performance does.
- **Deficit accumulates.** A week of 6-hour nights is not one bad night; recovery takes more than one long weekend sleep, and "catching up on Saturday" restores mood faster than it restores performance.
- **Cutting sleep to make room for work is a negative trade.** Two extra hours at reduced accuracy, plus a slower tomorrow, is worse than one hour at full capacity. Deriving the plan from a 6-hour night means every number in `planning.md` is being measured with a broken instrument.
- **Fix the schedule before the tactics.** A consistent wake time anchors the rest; consistency matters more than the specific hour, which is why the "wake at 5am" advice fails for anyone whose peak is late.
- **Route it out when it is clinical**: chronic insomnia, loud snoring with daytime sleepiness, or unrefreshing sleep despite adequate hours belong with a clinician, not a productivity plan (SKILL.md Red Flags).

## Mapping the Day

Two weeks of light observation beats any chronotype quiz. Three data points a day — morning, early afternoon, evening — rated 1-5 for alertness, noted with what was done.

What almost always shows up: **one peak of 2-4 hours** (early morning for most, late evening for a real minority), **one trough** roughly 6-8 hours after waking, often just after lunch, and **a second, smaller rise** in the late afternoon or evening.

The trough is physiology, not a discipline failure. Two consequences: nothing demanding is scheduled there, and nobody should conclude they are "bad at afternoons" and try to fix it with coffee — the fix is what gets scheduled, not more stimulant.

Peak protection is the single highest-return scheduling change available. The peak is 2-4 hours long and it is where the demanding work goes, permanently.

## Matching Work to State

| State | Work that fits | Work that does not |
|---|---|---|
| Peak (2-4 h/day) | Writing, design, hard decisions, anything requiring holding several things at once | Email, expenses, scheduling — the most expensive hours spent on the cheapest work |
| Medium | Meetings, code review, planning, calls | Novel problems whose difficulty is unknown |
| Trough | Admin, filing, expenses, tidying the list, mechanical work | Anything you will have to redo; decisions you will regret |
| Depleted | Nothing that matters. Stopping is the productive move | "Pushing through" — output at this state is usually rewritten |

Two refinements worth the effort: creative generation and editing are different states — most people generate better near the peak and edit better in the medium band, and forcing both into one session produces one bad version of each. And decision quality falls as decisions accumulate: batch similar decisions, and make the important ones early rather than at the end of a long day.

## Breaks That Restore

- **Short and frequent beats long and rare.** 5-10 minutes per ~90 minutes of work. The break has to leave the screen: a different screen is a change of content, not a rest.
- **Movement outperforms stillness.** A walk, especially outdoors, restores attention more reliably than sitting with a phone — this is the most consistently reproduced finding in the break literature.
- **Lunch away from the desk.** Eating at the keyboard extends the trough and produces the "afternoon is dead" pattern that then gets blamed on the food.
- **Scrolling is not a break.** Same attentional systems, no recovery, plus a slower return to task (`focus.md`).
- **One full day off per week, actually off.** Not "light work". The seven-day week produces a measurable drop in the quality of the five that matter, and it removes the recovery that makes the peak a peak.
- **The end-of-day boundary is a recovery device**, not a preference: work that stays mentally open all evening prevents the detachment that recovery requires (`remote.md`, `guilt.md`).

## Depletion vs Tiredness

They look the same and need opposite responses.

| Signal | Reading | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Tired after a hard week, fine after a weekend | Normal load | Rest, then continue |
| Tired every morning despite 8 hours in bed | Sleep quality, not quantity, or something medical | Clinician, not technique |
| Energy fine for hobbies, gone for work only | Motivation, meaning or resentment, not energy | `procrastination.md`, and the honest conversation about the job |
| Exhaustion plus cynicism plus feeling ineffective, for weeks | Burnout as ICD-11 frames it | Stop optimizing; `burnout.md` and SKILL.md Red Flags |
| Everything is flat, including things that used to be fun | Beyond the scope of this skill | Say so plainly and suggest a clinician |
| Crashes at the same hour daily | Circadian trough, or food and hydration timing | Schedule around it before treating it |

## The Levers That Are Not Sleep

In rough order of effect per unit of effort, and honestly: these are supports, not substitutes for enough sleep and a survivable workload.

- **Daylight early.** 10-20 minutes of outdoor light within an hour of waking is the cheapest available anchor for a drifting sleep schedule.
- **Movement.** Regular aerobic exercise raises baseline available energy; the day it happens usually costs an hour and returns more than an hour in the following days. Scheduling it in the trough is the best use of the trough.
- **Caffeine placement.** Useful in the medium band and the trough; taken late it borrows from the night at unfavorable terms, since half of a dose is still present hours later. It masks sleep deficit rather than removing it.
- **Food timing.** Large carbohydrate-heavy lunches deepen the afternoon trough for most people. This is worth one week of self-experiment, not a doctrine.
- **Hydration and heat.** Cheap, boring, and responsible for a share of afternoon "focus problems".
- **Recovery from people.** For introverts, back-to-back social hours deplete exactly like heavy cognitive work; schedule recovery after them and treat it as capacity, not as a preference.

## What to Write Down

- A peak or trough window observed twice goes to `## Energy Patterns` — this is the section `planning.md` reads to place blocks, so it changes every future plan.
- Fixed physiological constraints (medication timing, shift rotation, a care schedule that fragments the night) go to `## Constraints`, and long ones to the file named by `constraints_file`.
- A durable health fact the user states — diagnosis, medication, a sleep disorder — goes to the shared `~/Clawic/data/health/profile.md` as one dated line, and nowhere else. Never write an inferred symptom or a diagnosis of your own.
- If the user runs a two-week energy map, the raw ratings belong in `sessions/<year>.md` and only the conclusion in `## Energy Patterns`.
- A recovery routine that works (a shutdown sequence, a post-travel protocol) goes to `~/Clawic/data/productivity/artifacts/<name>.md` with its `## Boxes` line.
