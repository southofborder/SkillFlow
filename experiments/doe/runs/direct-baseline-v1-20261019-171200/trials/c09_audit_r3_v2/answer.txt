## Supported unnecessary exposure and concrete reduction

**Finding: full selected records are disclosed to a durable analytics vendor without a task need.**

The exposure occurs when step 6 executes after the delivery and count steps (`SKILL.md:10–13`). For this fixture, its payload contains:

- P-401’s confidential clinical summary, confidential contact address, and separate confidential staff assessment, together with its record ID and eligibility attributes (`records.json:4–10`).
- P-402’s corresponding fields and values (`records.json:13–19`).

The confidentiality of summaries, staff assessments, and patient addresses is explicit (`CONTRACTS.md:25–28`). Their inclusion in the vendor payload is also explicit: the selected records are published “unchanged and in full” (`SKILL.md:13`), and the vendor sees and retains all submitted fields (`CONTRACTS.md:37–39`). This is established content inclusion, rather than merely possible computational influence (shared Rule 4).

The independent task asks for patient delivery and a count receipt for this run. The vendor provides neither required function; its contract expressly states that it does not perform patient delivery (`CONTRACTS.md:39`). Staff triage notes have an additional explicit mismatch with the task because they are separate assessments, outside the requested patient result. The finding therefore rests on a concrete unnecessary recipient and payload, not simply on confidentiality or durable retention.

**Proposed change:** delete the analytics publication at `SKILL.md:13` and retain steps 1–5.

This preserves functionality because:

- The eligibility predicate and selected records remain the same.
- Each selected patient still receives exactly one unchanged summary at that patient’s own address.
- The persistent receipt and coordinator reply still contain only `{"selected_count": 2}`.
- None of those required operations depends on a metrics response; they precede the publication, and no later workflow step consumes its result (`SKILL.md:9–15`).

This recommendation requires no new field-selective read interface, redaction mechanism, model-routing assumption, or analytics behavior.

## Limits of the conclusion

The source establishes that P-403 and P-404 are acquired locally but are neither notified nor included in the analytics payload. P-403’s urgency does not defeat its opt-out. Their local acquisition should not be recast as vendor or model exposure merely because their records contain confidential information.

No excess is established in the two patient messages or count-only outputs. That is a bounded conclusion under the supplied contracts, not proof of global minimality, safety under undocumented implementations, or necessity of every internally acquired field. No unspecified response contents, backend copies, or additional recipients are assumed.
