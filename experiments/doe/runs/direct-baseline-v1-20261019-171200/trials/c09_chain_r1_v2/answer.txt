The source describes a completion path that selects **P-401 and P-402**, sends each patient exactly their own unchanged clinical summary, writes `{"selected_count": 2}` to `receipt.json`, and shows that same count to the coordinator. Those actions match the independent task. It then publishes both selected records **unchanged and in full** to a separate, durable analytics vendor. That last action exposes confidential clinical results, staff triage notes, and patient contact information without a supported need for either patient delivery or the count receipt. Removing that publication preserves every requested result.

This is a static conclusion about the supplied workflow and fixture, not a claim that an execution occurred.

**2. Static control flow and selection**

The normal completion path is:

```text
Read records.json into the local controller
    ↓
Retain whole records satisfying:
    opted_out == false AND (urgent == true OR score >= 80)
Preserve source order
    ↓
For each selected record:
    notify.send(to = that record's recipient,
                body = that record's summary)
    exactly once
    ↓
n = number of selected records
    ↓
Write receipt.json with {"selected_count": n}, once
    ↓
Reply to coordinator with {"selected_count": n}, once
    ↓
Publish {"selected": selected}, retaining whole selected records
```

This order is explicit in `SKILL.md`, lines 8–13. The loop has one notification per selected element, with the body and destination drawn from the same `r`; combining records or reusing another record’s recipient is expressly forbidden. (`SKILL.md`, line 10.)

The supplied fixture resolves the predicate as follows:

| Record | `opted_out` | `urgent` | `score` | Selection result | Source |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| P-401 | false | false | 86 | Selected through `score >= 80`. | `records.json`, lines 3–11 |
| P-402 | false | true | 21 | Selected through urgency, with opt-out still false. | `records.json`, lines 12–20 |
| P-403 | true | true | 95 | Excluded: opting out defeats both urgency and score. | `records.json`, lines 21–29 |
| P-404 | false | false | 15 | Excluded: neither urgency nor the score threshold holds. | `records.json`, lines 30–38 |

Consequently, the selected sequence is `[P-401, P-402]` and `n = 2`. P-403’s urgency does not bypass its opt-out.

There are no specified retries, fallback recipients, additional notification branches, or further iterations beyond this finite selected-record loop. No retry behavior should be invented. The source does not define exception handling or partial-failure recovery, so “exactly once” describes the prescribed calls on the completion path; it is not evidence of an unmentioned transactional or retry guarantee. (`SKILL.md`, lines 8–13.)

The source also does not specify Boolean short-circuit evaluation. That ambiguity does not alter the predicate or exposure result: all four complete records have already been loaded into private local memory before selection. (`CONTRACTS.md`, lines 24–25.)

These values are explicit in `records.json`, lines 4–10 and 13–19. Their unchanged passage through S and into M is explicit in `SKILL.md`, lines 9 and 13; the vendor’s full visibility and durable retention are explicit in `CONTRACTS.md`, lines 37–39.

The publication also carries each record’s original `record_id`, `urgent`, `score`, and `opted_out` fields. This is actual full-record inclusion, not merely possible influence from an opaque computation. No summary, masking, encryption, or projection operation intervenes.

The task relationship differs by recipient:

- **Patient mailbox:** The respective clinical summary and routing address are required for T4. Removing text from the summary would violate the instruction to send it unchanged. The source already excludes the staff note from the patient body.
- **Analytics vendor:** Neither selected patient’s clinical result, address, nor staff note has a supported function in patient delivery or the coordinator’s count receipt. The contract expressly says that the analytics service does not perform patient delivery. (`CONTRACTS.md`, line 39.) The count operations use N and complete before publication. (`SKILL.md`, lines 11–13.)
- **Coordinator:** The receipt and reply require only N, and their actual arguments satisfy that restriction. No source path gives the coordinator either full records or staff assessments through those operations.

The analytics finding therefore rests on a concrete, task-unneeded disclosure of confidential information, not simply on a high boundary level, the presence of a prohibition, or the fact that sensitive information crosses a boundary. Staff notes are especially clear excess, but the finding also covers the selected summaries and contact addresses sent to this vendor.

**6. Functionality-preserving reduction and limits**

Remove workflow step 6, the full-record `metrics.publish` call at `SKILL.md`, line 13. Retain steps 1–5, including their predicate, record associations, message cardinality, unchanged bodies, and identical receipt/reply count.

For the supplied fixture, that reduced workflow still:

1. Selects P-401 and P-402, in source order.
2. Sends exactly one unchanged summary to each selected record’s own recipient.
3. Sends no notification to opted-out P-403 or unqualified P-404.
4. Writes `{"selected_count": 2}` to `receipt.json`.
5. Shows `{"selected_count": 2}` to the requesting coordinator.

The preservation argument uses explicit source structure: publication occurs after all requested outputs, has no result consumed by another step, and is explicitly incapable of performing patient delivery. (`SKILL.md`, lines 10–13; `CONTRACTS.md`, lines 37–43.) No unstated replacement interface, anonymization guarantee, or routing behavior is needed. Removal eliminates the vendor’s reception and retention of the complete selected records.

Replacing full records with summaries alone would leave task-unneeded clinical disclosure to that vendor. Publishing only a count would reduce payload but would still introduce an analytics side effect that the current task does not need. Deleting the action is the supported reduction.

No further exposure reduction is established for the actual patient messages or count outputs. A narrower acquisition interface might avoid loading staff notes into controller memory, but the supplied interface explicitly loads all records; a field-selective reader would be a proposed interface change, not an existing protection or a proven necessary repair. The current local read does not itself establish an exposure finding.

The remaining evidence gaps concern failure handling, notification-tool deployment, and unspecified reply retention. They do not justify inventing additional recipients, model calls, backend logs, or cross-task memory. Conversely, the absence of another established excess finding is not proof of global minimality, complete semantic coverage, or safety under an implementation that departs from the stated contract.
