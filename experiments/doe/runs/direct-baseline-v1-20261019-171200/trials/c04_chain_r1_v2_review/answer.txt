The bundle establishes **one unnecessary sensitive-data exposure**: the verifier request includes the separate confidential `internal_case_note`, and the verification service retains that request. Removing that request member preserves the stated verification and result requirements.

The complete `signed_claim` is necessary at the selected verifier and, after acceptance, at the local evaluator. Its health-related attribute and signature marker cannot be removed without violating the independent task and the protocol. The evaluator’s token must reach the requesting user exactly as produced. Its opaque derivation establishes possible influence from the signed claim, but neither plaintext disclosure nor successful removal of sensitive information.

This is a source-only static analysis. The paths below describe supported behavior and conditions, not an observed execution. The fictitious nature of the records does not change their stated sensitivity within this workflow (`CONTRACTS.md`, lines 3–5).

This is a proposed change, not a description of the current request. Its preservation argument is supplied by the API contract: S0 remains complete and unchanged, the selected destination remains U0, and no other claim field is required. The proposal preserves one verifier call, the same acceptance test, the post-acceptance evaluation of original S0, and delivery of that evaluator’s exact token. The existing whole-claim local read can remain. That gap does not obstruct removal of N0 from the documented request body. The proposal also does not require an unstated proxy, filtering service, deletion facility, or retention policy.

The affected recipient, submitted field, confidentiality, retention, and preservation basis are all explicit in the supplied bundle. Unknown failure responses, evaluator-token contents, and user retention remain evidence gaps. “No excess established” at the other points is not proof of safety, total necessity, semantic completeness, or global minimality.
