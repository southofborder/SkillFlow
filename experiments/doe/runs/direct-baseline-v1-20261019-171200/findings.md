**Scientific interpretation of the direct strong-model baseline**

The completed experiment demonstrates source-only replacement capability on this limited set of explicit-contract cases, **including full semantic-chain reconstruction**. All 36 held-out first answers for direct DOE audit and all 36 for full semantic chains satisfied the paired exploratory reference assessments. Each arm also had 12 of 12 independently reviewed answers satisfy those assessments. Every one of the 12 held-out cases passed on all three first-answer repeats in each arm. **The current experiment has not established SkillFlow's actual empirical advantage.** There was no same-model SkillFlow control, so the results cannot establish either architecture's relative accuracy, cost, or reliability. They also do not establish universal replacement beyond these materials. This interpretation follows the [rules declared before held-out trials](interpretation-rules.json), rather than treating a favorable baseline as evidence for an unmeasured architectural benefit.

The relevant goal is task-related static sensitive-data minimization: identify unnecessary sensitive exposure both from unnecessary actions and from excess payload on necessary actions, while preserving required behavior, data versions, conditions, and recipients. A sensitive value reaching a sink is insufficient by itself to establish DOE. These are the project's [current DOE goals and design principles](../../../../docs/doe/SkillFlow-DOE目标与设计原则.md). The experiment tests whether a strong source-only model can provide the corresponding audit or reconstruct the full semantic argument; it does not execute the Skills or measure actual leaks.

The [preregistered design](preregistration.json) used two separate deliverables. The audit arm requested source-linked requirements, boundary coverage, task-relative judgments, and preserving reductions. The chain arm additionally required all five connected layers: task requirements; control flow; sources, relations, and data versions; propagation to supported receiving and storage points; and final sensitive-data minimization judgments. No SkillFlow-specific schema was required. Targets received complete source bundles, any independent user task, and a shared static-analysis contract, rather than historical CFGs, extracted Tasks, annotations, propagation results, DOE results, reference answers, or scores. The [final audit prompt](prompts/direct-audit-final.txt) and [final chain prompt](prompts/full-chain-final.txt) make that baseline reusable. “Source-only” describes the analytical evidence supplied; the target runtime's base instructions were still present.

The held-out cohort consists of 12 new assistant-authored synthetic cases arranged as four controlled triplets, with three first-answer repeats per case and deliverable. Only repeat 1 was preselected for an additional independent review. Three familiar pilots, 001, 010, and 013, were used for development. One minimal, uniform prompt revision was then frozen for both deliverables; no held-out-specific prompt revision or quality resampling was allowed. The 120 imported target calls comprise 96 held-out calls and 24 development calls across the original and revised pilots. The 72 superseded, unexecuted preparations are bookkeeping records, not additional model samples or failures.

These controlled contrasts cover necessary sensitive use, removable sensitive payload on required actions, and removable actions that disclose sensitive data. They also test per-record recipient pairing, exact predicates, bounded retry conditions, earlier versus later data versions, persistent memory, and opaque computation. Passing the full-chain arm means that a source-only prompt supported an assessed reconstruction of these connected semantics, not merely a plausible final DOE verdict. There is no observed held-out deficit in full-chain reconstruction that could justify reserving that capability as an established advantage of SkillFlow.

The score remains narrower than proof of semantic completeness. References were authored in separate source-only contexts before the target outputs, and two separate assessors checked the candidates, but target, reference authors, and graders used the same configured model. Their errors can be correlated. Trial identity, repeat number, and execution order were masked; the requested deliverable necessarily revealed artifact kind, and answer structure or correction notes could reveal review status. This is identity masking, not complete condition blinding. No independent human gold standard or human adjudication was used.

The [reference observations](reference-observations.json) expose a further measurement limit: for c01, one author itemized the two selected patients' disclosures separately while the other grouped the same field leakage across records. This is a difference in exposure units, not a substantive disagreement about the source. Some assessor exposure counts are also deliberately unset. This report therefore does not calculate pooled micro-precision, recall, or an exposure-level score from those divergent units. The paired answer-level outcomes and per-case repetitions are the supported aggregates. The four related triplets are not 12 independent real-world domains, and three repeats on each source do not provide a general population error rate.

The actual development errors are informative precisely because their scope is limited. In [case 013's source](cases/013/source_bundle.txt), `SKILL.md:13–14` requires returning the successful response's body unchanged or the archive error; it does not identify the caller as a user. Shared rule 6 explicitly says ordinary return does not automatically establish user delivery. Both the [original audit](trials/p013_audit_r1/answer.txt) and the [revised audit](trials/p013_audit_r1_v2/answer.txt) nevertheless classified terminal returns as user-output boundaries. The original additionally proposed relaying the result to the user; the revised answer repeated the premise that the successful body must reach the user. Both assessors rated each answer as a substantive error because recipient identity changes boundary inventory and the preservation argument.

That recipient error coexisted with substantial correct analysis. The two audits retained credential-present and absent branches, the transient-only single retry, the source-ID-only bounded archive fallback, unchanged terminal values, and the status append before every terminal return. Both identified possible acquisition-to-model observation of `FAST_KEY`, while preserving required credential delivery to `fast.fetch` and declining to infer credential plaintext in opaque results or status. The error was not a demonstrated extra credential leak at the imagined user boundary. It was an unsupported receiving-subject claim, independently material to the requested audit. The [raw ratings](assessment-summary.json) retain those distinctions; they do not support a new broad failure taxonomy.

The [uniform development revision](prompt-development-review.json) added only a source-only self-check asking both arms to recheck recipient claims and make unstated interface or routing premises conditional. It supplied no case-specific answer and left the shared rules and review template unchanged. The repeated 013 error shows that this reminder did not make that inference reliable in the one revised pilot draw: the answer qualified several other mechanisms yet retained the unsupported user recipient. It does not reveal the model's internal cause, prove that the reminder had no effect elsewhere, or establish a causal advantage for a different output structure. The original and revised full-chain pilots both passed all three cases, so a source-only structured prompt was already a viable alternative on these materials.

The [original independent review](trials/p013_audit_r1_review/answer.txt) and [revised independent review](trials/p013_audit_r1_v2_review/answer.txt) corrected the concrete mismatch. They retained ordinary returns with unresolved receiving endpoints and made any downstream user route conditional, while preserving the credential finding and bounded workflow. Both reviewed answers satisfied both assessors. The revised review also explicitly limited credential isolation to removing the direct acquisition-to-model path rather than claiming that unknown returned values could never reintroduce the credential. At the observable output level, a review of a concrete answer located and repaired a mismatch that a general generation reminder had not prevented. These two examples support a two-call alternative worth comparing: one source-only answer plus one independent source-grounded review. They do not establish a general repair probability or erase the two original errors. Held-out reviews preserved passing judgments, but there were no held-out first-answer failures from which to estimate error-repair effectiveness; repeats 2 and 3 were not reviewed.

Correct uncertainty handling is part of success here, not a claim of safety. For pilot 010, the sources do not supply concrete sensitive request/response contents or establish the ordinary return's downstream recipient. Declining to assert a sensitive DOE finding can therefore satisfy the source reference while retaining possible observation and unresolved content. For the signed-claim triplet, the required opaque evaluator token may depend on its input; that does not establish that it contains every input's plaintext or that it is sanitized. Similarly, a source-supported excess can be conditional on an unspecified retry or acceptance result without being an observed execution. “No excess established,” missing sensitivity evidence, reference satisfaction, and propagation completion are all distinct from proof of no DOE, global minimality, or natural-language semantic equivalence.

The [historical context review](historical-context.md) offers qualitative background only. For 001 and 013, the revised direct full-chain answers articulate conditional, receiver-specific minimization proposals for model observation of named credentials. The inspected historical deterministic results already contain the corresponding model-observation boundaries and credential sensitivity; they abstain under partial evidence or conservative demand limitations. Those are not newly discovered historical flow omissions or demonstrated historical model failures. For 010, no concrete sensitive-data difference is defensible. The historical pipeline requested a different model and effort, retained prior CFGs, used multiple stages, and produced different output types. It cannot supply the missing same-model end-to-end control or establish overall superiority of either approach.

Target usage was measured from 120 distinct response receipts with no missing core usage. It includes base runtime instructions and complete target prompts, not just the visible source text. Across all targets, including both pilot versions, usage was **3,984,744 input + 575,382 output = 4,560,126 total tokens**. The held-out cohort accounted for **3,625,252 tokens**; original and revised development accounted for the remaining 934,874. Cached input and reasoning-output fields are subsets and are not added again. Reference authoring, scoring, root orchestration, and engineering audits are excluded. No currency estimate is available or inferred.

Engineering checks support the integrity of these observations without certifying their semantics. The [engineering audit](engineering-report.md) verified `gpt-6.1-sol` with `ultra` effort for all 120 targets, canonical identities, single attributed target turns, and no observed target tool use. Tools were prohibited by protocol, not disabled by a hard sandbox. Actual dispatcher invocation metadata verified explicit `fork_turns=none` on all 120 calls and agreement with the frozen effective schedule. The child `forked_from_id` field was absent in all 120 receipts, rather than explicitly null; that absence alone proves neither fresh history nor a violation. Each child had one observed incoming agent message, but collaboration payloads are encrypted in the local receipts, so exact plaintext prompt delivery equality and absence of additional content inside the encrypted payload cannot be independently verified.

The engineering audit's exact-receipt replay covered all 120 targets with zero errors. The subsequent [final zero-model-call replay](final-replay.json), reproducible with the [saved audit script](final-replay-audit.py), checked 120 paired candidates and 240 individual scores, 196 blind-cohort hash comparisons, two protocol hashes, 15 current tooling hashes, and 15 snapshot-copy comparisons. All 1,170 protected files were unchanged, and deterministic regeneration reproduced the hashes of six summary/report files. The existing [26 focused tests](focused-tests.json) also passed. These results support preserved bytes, receipt attribution, aggregation, and reproducible reporting. They do not reproduce model generation or re-prove the source interpretations, preservation arguments, or reference completeness.

SkillFlow's remaining value propositions are therefore hypotheses to measure. Deterministic propagation might improve mechanical consistency across reused facts; persisted identities and versions might support reuse across edits; programmatic processing might scale to larger Skills; and structured evidence might reduce human review effort. None of those comparative benefits was measured here. The current direct baseline also demonstrates that a model can generate structured chains, so the mere presence of a graph, more fields, or an explicit chain cannot by itself substantiate an advantage. A future benefit must be demonstrated as improved quality, lower cost, greater scale, or less review work under comparable conditions.

The next discriminating experiment should compare the complete current SkillFlow pipeline with the direct audit and full-chain baselines, using the same model and effort, equal complete source packages, equal independent tasks, and matched runtime assumptions. It should include both one-answer and one-answer-plus-review direct workflows, with development and test material separated and budgets or quality/cost trade-offs declared in advance. Human-validated difficult real Skills should test ambiguous contracts, larger cross-file flows, conditions, versions, recipients, and preservation requirements rather than relying only on closely related synthetic variants. Common output obligations and source-grounded human adjudication should measure missed supported excess, unsupported findings or boundaries, functionality-breaking reductions, unresolved cases, and cross-layer consistency. End-to-end token use, comparable wall time, intervention frequency, and human review effort should be recorded alongside quality. SkillFlow's own extraction, review, repair, and failed-run costs must be included, just as the direct two-call workflow includes its original answer. Reuse and scale should be tested explicitly if they are claimed benefits. Until that comparison exists, the supported finding remains successful source-only audit and full-chain capability on this bounded exploratory set, with SkillFlow's actual advantage unestablished.
