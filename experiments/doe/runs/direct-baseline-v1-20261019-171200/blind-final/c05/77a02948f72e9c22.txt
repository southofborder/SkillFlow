The stated workflow supports **two task-required patient deliveries and a receipt count of 2**. It does not support a finding of unnecessary sensitive-data exposure: the clinical summaries reach their own patients unchanged; the receipt and coordinator reply contain only the selected count; and staff triage notes remain inside the explicitly private, ephemeral controller. This is a conclusion about the supplied workflow, fixture, and execution contract—not proof of global minimality or safety under an unspecified deployment.

The values are fictitious, but the analysis treats clinical summaries, staff assessments, and patient addresses as confidential as instructed by `CONTRACTS.md:3–5, 24–30`.

**Static control flow.** The following is a representation of the source, not an observed execution:

```text
Read all records into private local memory.
    |
Retain each whole record satisfying:
    opted_out == false AND (urgent == true OR score >= 80)
Preserve source order.
    |
For each retained record, in that order:
    call notify.send(to = that record's recipient,
                     body = that record's summary)
    exactly once
    |
n = length(selected)
    |
Write receipt.json once with {"selected_count": n}.
    |
Reply once with {"selected_count": n}.
    |
End of the listed workflow.
```

This ordering and the loop are explicit in `SKILL.md:8–12`. There is no additional delivery branch, retry, analytics call, context-save operation, or model call in the workflow. There is no bounded retry count to preserve: no retry is specified.

**Task-related sensitive-data minimization judgments.**

1. **The two unchanged clinical summaries are necessary sensitive use at their respective patient mailboxes.** T4 requires the entire summary unchanged, so removing the embedded patient identifier, shortening clinical instructions, or rewriting either string would not preserve the requested function. The payloads contain exactly the required summary versions, without triage-note additions (`SKILL.md:10`; `CONTRACTS.md:32–35`; `records.json:6, 15`).

2. **Each selected patient’s address is necessary routing information for that patient’s delivery.** The address remains paired with its own summary and is used only for delivery under the contract. There is no supported transfer of one patient’s result to another patient (`SKILL.md:10`; `CONTRACTS.md:27–35`). A separate external notification implementation remains unresolved as described above; current use alone is not proof that every possible processor arrangement is minimal.

3. **Staff triage notes are acquired, but unnecessary disclosure of them is not established.** All four are loaded locally, and the selected list retains the P-401 and P-402 notes as part of their whole records (`records.json:7, 16, 25, 34`; `SKILL.md:8–9`). None reaches B1–B4 because notification bodies select `summary` and count bodies select `n`. Explicit controller isolation prevents treating this broader acquisition as model exposure (`CONTRACTS.md:7–21`). Calling these notes “outside the requested result” does not itself turn private local acquisition into a disclosure sink.

4. **P-403 and P-404 have no supported patient-delivery boundary.** Their exclusion follows the actual predicate. In particular, P-403’s urgency and high score do not bypass opting out (`SKILL.md:9–10`; `records.json:21–38`). Their sensitive fields remain local rather than being copied into a receipt or another patient’s message.

5. **The count is the requested aggregate at both coordinator-facing points.** It depends on selection outcomes, but it is not a transfer of the underlying records or selection fields. B3 and B4 contain only `{"selected_count": 2}`, and both use the same `n` (`SKILL.md:11–12`; `CONTRACTS.md:41–43`). An aggregate can reveal information about a run, but this aggregate is expressly requested; no excess count-related payload is present.

No exposure-reduction repair is warranted by the supported boundary payloads. Removing a selected notification, altering its summary, changing its recipient, or removing the count would break an explicit positive requirement. There is also no supported analytics delivery, model observation, or triage-note output to remove.

A narrower local representation could optionally project selected records to their unchanged `recipient` and `summary` fields after selection, preserving order and deriving the same count. That proposal would preserve all listed downstream operations because those operations use only these two fields and the list length (`SKILL.md:10–12`). It would reduce incidental local retention of other fields in `selected`, but it would **not** reduce the initial whole-record acquisition, remove the original `records` collection, or remedy an established exposure. A claim that initial acquisition could be narrowed would require an additional documented read interface; none is supplied.

The layers are consistent: the predicate yields precisely P-401 and P-402; both notification bodies and recipients come from their own retained elements; opting out excludes P-403 despite urgency; triage notes stop in ephemeral state; and the same derived count, 2, reaches the receipt and coordinator reply. Remaining gaps concern notification deployment and transport, unspecified recipient retention, and failure handling. Those gaps limit the conclusion without supporting a forced positive finding or an all-clear claim.
