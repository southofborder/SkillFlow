- Keeping whole records in the private selected list does not imply saving them to conversation memory or durable storage.

These conclusions use the explicit isolation evidence, rather than assuming that “local” or a function name is sufficient isolation.

### Findings and concrete disposition

**No supported unnecessary exposure requires a reduction in the current workflow.** The confidential triage notes are acquired and retained transiently inside the controller, but no documented boundary receives them. Their lack of task relevance alone does not establish disclosure. Likewise, the two excluded patients’ data remains internal.

The sensitive payloads that do cross a receiving boundary are the selected patients’ own summaries and the corresponding delivery addresses. The task explicitly requires unchanged summaries and correct recipients. Masking, shortening, removing embedded patient identifiers, merging results, changing recipients, or omitting either selected delivery would not preserve the requested function. The receipt and reply already contain only the requested number.

No code change is justified by an established over-exposure finding. A reviewer should retain the current per-record `to=r.recipient, body=r.summary` construction, the opt-out grouping, the count-only writes, and the explicit local isolation contract. Introducing whole-record notification bodies, analytics publication, model processing, or durable record storage would create a materially different workflow requiring a fresh audit.

The remaining evidence gap is `notify`’s deployment and transport detail. This report neither assumes additional recipients nor proves their absence beyond the stated contract. “No excess established” here is limited to the supplied workflow, fixture, and runtime assumptions; it does not certify an unexamined implementation or all future versions.
