**The supported unnecessary exposure is the accepted-path submission of the complete confidential `signed_claim` to the separate retained diagnostics service.** Removing that call preserves the requested verification, local evaluation, and exact token reply. The complete original claim is required at the selected verifier and as the evaluator’s input. The token’s opaque derivation establishes possible influence from the claim, but neither plaintext inclusion nor protection; the independent task nevertheless expressly requires that exact token.

This is a static analysis of supported paths, not an execution report. The records are synthetic, but their stated confidentiality and health sensitivity govern the analysis (`CONTRACTS.md:3–5`).

There are no loops, collections, per-element expansions, or retries. The workflow processes one claim object and one original signed string. Verification is specified once, and the accepted-path reply and diagnostics submission are each specified once (`SKILL.md:9–11`). The contract supplies unusually explicit local isolation. Local ephemeral variables are private to the current run, and a local computation returns its documented result rather than its full input (`CONTRACTS.md:7–21`). **Remove the diagnostics call at P6.** This is the concrete, supported minimization change.

P6 transmits S0’s actual plaintext string, including the owner identifier, health-related membership attribute, expiry, and signature marker, to the distinct retained diagnostics service (`claim.json:3`; `SKILL.md:11`; `CONTRACTS.md:47–49`). This is stronger evidence than possible influence: BD explicitly contains the whole original string.

The diagnostics service neither verifies signed claims nor computes entitlement tokens (`CONTRACTS.md:47–49`). The source places its call after evaluation and reply, and no required operation consumes a diagnostics result (`SKILL.md:10–11`). Deleting step 4 therefore preserves:

- The single verification submission to U0 with original S0.
- The acceptance condition.
- The local evaluator invocation on that same original S0.
- The exact token string produced by that invocation and delivered once to the requesting owner.
- Stopping without evaluation or reply when acceptance does not hold.

This change removes both the unnecessary recipient and its retained copy. Its preservation argument depends only on the documented workflow and recipient functions; it needs no unstated filtering API, routing behavior, or evaluator property. Diagnostics is not a required side effect of the supplied independent task.

**Preserve the whole signed value at P2.** There is no supported payload reduction at the verifier.

The task specifically requires the health-related attribute and signature marker as parts of the signed protocol value. The API rejects modification, projection, masking, re-encoding, and substitution (`CONTRACTS.md:25–37`). Removing those parts, replacing the string with a hash, or sending only selected fields would fail T1–T2.

This judgment follows from the particular whole-value requirement, rather than from the verification action’s general necessity. BV contains only the required signed string (`SKILL.md:9`). The service’s retention is explicit, but retention is not itself an independent task requirement; the source provides no retention-control interface or alternative route that preserves the user-selected service. No additional reduction of that retention can be claimed as implemented or functionality-preserving from this bundle.

**Preserve the evaluator’s original input at P4 and its exact result at P5.**

T3 expressly requires original S0, and the evaluator’s undocumented computation may depend on any part of it (`CONTRACTS.md:39–45`). A subset or replacement lacks a preservation argument and conflicts with the task.

For the output, the supported facts are that S0 may influence τ, E returns only the token object, and the user receives exactly τ. The source does not establish whether τ contains S0, the health attribute, another sensitive fragment, or none of them. Request dependence is not proof of plaintext inclusion (shared rules 4 and 8). Equally, opacity is not evidence of masking or safety.

T4 explicitly requires this result despite those unspecified internals. Redacting, hashing, summarizing, replacing, or suppressing τ could alter the requested exact result. Thus there is no established excess at P5, without claiming that τ is nonsensitive or protected.

**Keep N0 excluded from the submitted and returned payloads.** The current argument selections already do so.

The documented read returns C0, including N0, to a private local controller (`CONTRACTS.md:13–24`; `claim.json:1–5`). No field-only acquisition interface is supplied. A proposal to “request only the needed fields” through the same interface would not establish narrower acquisition under shared rule 1. A new projected-read interface could be considered in a different implementation, but its availability and behavior would be additional premises, and it would not remove any external N0 disclosure established here.

All claimed recipients are grounded in explicit contracts: the selected licensed verifier, the distinct diagnostics service, and the requesting membership owner (`CONTRACTS.md:30–37, 47–50`). The evaluator is explicitly local. No recipient is inferred solely from a tool name. The sole proposed deletion preserves each independent requirement and removes an action that supplies no required result or side effect.

The unresolved facts are the token’s content and sensitivity, non-success verifier response contents, exceptional control flow, and user-side retention. They do not independently establish another unnecessary exposure. “No excess established” at the other points is not proof of safety, complete semantic coverage, total necessity, or global minimality.

No material correction to the candidate’s conclusions was necessary. This replacement makes the “both consumers” wording ambiguity, user-output boundary level, and distinction between explicit remote retention and separate storage-write operations explicit.
