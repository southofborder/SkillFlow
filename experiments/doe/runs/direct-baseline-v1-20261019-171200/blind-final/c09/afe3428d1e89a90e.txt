**Static control flow and selection**

The workflow is a linear sequence with one filter and one per-record loop:

```text

    ↓
Retain each record satisfying:
    opted_out == false AND (urgent == true OR score >= 80)
Preserve source order.
    ↓
For each retained record:
    send its own unchanged summary to its own recipient, once.
    ↓
Compute n = number of retained records.
    ↓
Write receipt.json with {"selected_count": n}, once.
    ↓
Show the coordinator {"selected_count": n}, once.
    ↓
Publish {"selected": selected} to the analytics vendor.
    ↓
End of the documented sequence.
```

**Sensitive-data judgments and reductions**

**Finding F1: Publishing the complete selected records exposes confidential patient and staff information to a vendor without a task need.**

The concrete source-to-boundary paths are:

```text
records.json P-401 and P-402
    → local read R
    → filter retaining full objects S
    → {"selected": S}
    → metrics vendor, which sees and durably retains every submitted field
```

The affected confidential data includes both selected patients’ clinical summaries, recipient addresses, and staff triage notes. Their identifiers and predicate fields are also submitted as associated record contents. The publication condition is normal advancement to the final workflow step, using the same S that drove the patient loop and count. It does not include P-403 or P-404 (`SKILL.md:8–13`; `CONTRACTS.md:24–39`; `records.json:3–38`).

This is unnecessary exposure relative to U1–U6 because:

- The patient-delivery requirement is already implemented by the per-record notification calls.
- The count receipt and reported count are already implemented by the preceding write and reply.
- The analytics service expressly does not perform patient delivery (`CONTRACTS.md:37–39`).
- No task requirement asks the vendor to receive results, contact information, staff assessments, or selected-record copies.
- The triage notes are expressly separate from the requested patient results under both U6 and `CONTRACTS.md:25–27`.

The finding does not rest solely on confidentiality, vendor access, retention, or the exclusion of staff notes from patient results. It rests on the absence of a contribution from this additional publication to the independently specified outcome, together with the explicit transfer of confidential fields.

**Proposed reduction:** Remove `SKILL.md` step 6, the call to:

```text
metrics.publish(body={"selected": selected})
```

Keep the read, predicate, source order, paired notification loop, count computation, receipt write, and coordinator reply unchanged.

**Function-preservation argument:** The publication occurs after all required operations. Nothing in the supplied workflow consumes its return value or uses it to determine eligibility, delivery, the receipt, or the reply. Removing it preserves the two correct unchanged-summary deliveries and both count outputs of `2`, while eliminating the complete vendor transfer. This argument depends only on the supplied sequence and contracts, not on an unstated filtering or routing interface (`SKILL.md:8–16`; `CONTRACTS.md:32–43`).

Sending only the count to analytics would reduce this payload but would retain an additional action with no requirement in the independent task. Removing the call provides the supported reduction without needing to invent an analytics obligation.

**Necessary confidential use at the patient-delivery boundaries**

For P-401 and P-402, the source selects exactly each record’s `summary` and its own `recipient`. The service adds no other record fields, does not combine records, uses the address only for delivery, and retains the summary’s characters unchanged (`SKILL.md:10`; `CONTRACTS.md:32–35`).

Consequently:

- Each selected patient receives exactly their requested clinical result.
- Neither selected patient receives the other patient’s result.
- Neither triage note is included in a patient notification.
- The excluded records produce no notification calls.

Removing sensitive text from these summaries would violate the explicit unchanged-summary requirement. Their record identifiers, as embedded in the summaries, therefore cannot be removed while preserving U3. No excess is established on these necessary notification payloads; that conclusion follows from their actual contents and destination pairing, not merely from the action being necessary.

**Authorized count outputs**

The receipt and coordinator reply each contain exactly `{"selected_count": 2}`. They disclose the selected cardinality requested by U4, without incorporating record fields (`SKILL.md:11–12`; `CONTRACTS.md:41–43`). Their dependence on clinical eligibility decisions does not establish inclusion of clinical-result plaintext. No reduction to these outputs is warranted by the supplied task.

**Local acquisition and unresolved implementation possibilities**

The workflow acquires the full records, including excluded patients’ confidential fields and all triage notes. The documented local routing keeps that acquisition private to the controller, and the rest of the variables remain ephemeral (`CONTRACTS.md:7–25`; `SKILL.md:15–16`). This is relevant lineage, but it is not an additional supported exposure sink.

A local projection could reduce which fields are carried forward after eligibility evaluation, but it would not undo the full acquisition. The source provides no field-selective read interface, so replacing the read with a narrower acquisition would require an explicit interface change. Neither option is needed for the supported fix: removing the analytics call eliminates the established unnecessary exposure while preserving the documented task behavior.

The remaining evidence limits are specific: runtime success and failure handling are unspecified; notification transport and infrastructure beyond the stated mailbox semantics are unspecified; coordinator-reply retention is unspecified; and the independent sensitivity of record identifiers and predicate metadata is not expressly classified. None of those gaps is needed to establish F1. Conversely, the absence of additional established excess does not prove global minimality, semantic completeness, or safety outside the supplied source and contract.
