**6. Sensitive-data minimization judgments and preservation limits**

**J1 — The complete signed claim at the verifier is necessary sensitive use, with no supported excess in its payload.**  
P2 exposes confidential owner and health-related information, as well as the remaining signed protocol string, to a retained service. That is a real sensitive-data boundary. Its necessity follows from the independent task’s explicit whole-value requirement and selected recipient, reinforced by the API’s exact-value requirement (`CONTRACTS.md:30–37`). It does not follow merely from the action being necessary.

The payload assessment is separate: `B0` includes only `S0`, and the entire `S0` is expressly required. Removing the health attribute, signature marker, or other characters; masking the string; projecting fields; or substituting a hash would violate the required protocol and result. Those are not functionality-preserving reductions. No additional sensitive body field or duplicate submission is supported by `SKILL.md:9–12`.

**J2 — The staff note has broader local acquisition but no established exposure.**  
`N0` is confidential, unrelated staff context and is outside the requested submission and result. Nevertheless, the actual local read returns the claim container, so the controller acquires it at P1 (`CONTRACTS.md:24,28`; `claim.json:4`). The explicit request construction excludes it, and the evaluator receives only `S0` (`SKILL.md:9–10`). The local isolation contract prevents inferring an automatic model observation.

A narrower reader could potentially reduce local access, but no such interface is documented. Claiming that the current getter can return only selected fields would introduce an unstated premise. The supported conclusion is limited: the note is acquired locally and is not traced to an exposure sink. Its mere presence in the input does not establish DOE.

**J3 — The evaluator’s possible influence is preserved without treating it as proven plaintext disclosure.**  
At P4, any part of `S0` may affect `T0`, including the health attribute. The contract leaves the token’s contents and protective properties unresolved (`CONTRACTS.md:41–45`). It would be incorrect either to label the token safe because it is called a “token” or to assert that it contains the whole claim because it depends on that input, under shared rule 4.

This local computation is explicitly required after acceptance, and the contract gives it no exposure effects. No unnecessary sensitive exposure is established at the evaluator itself.

**J4 — Returning the exact token is required; its opacity does not establish removable excess.**  
P5 delivers only `T0`, not `C0`, `N0`, the request body, or an automatic input wrapper (`CONTRACTS.md:39–45,50`; `SKILL.md:10`). The independent task specifically requires that exact token despite its unspecified internals. Even if a concrete token retained sensitive claim characters, that possibility alone would not establish unnecessary content relative to this task.

Truncating, masking, replacing, or recomputing the token would not preserve the exact-result obligation. The supplied material provides no warranted token-reduction proposal. This is a necessity judgment about the required returned value, not a claim that the token is irreversible or free of sensitive information.

Every claimed recipient has a cited delivery contract, and no reduction above relies on an assumed filtering interface or routing behavior. The unresolved issues remain the evaluator token’s actual contents, unspecified failure behavior, and user-side retention. Within those limits, the source supports the two required sensitive-data paths and no supported unnecessary exposure to remove.
