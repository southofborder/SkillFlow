# Constraint acceptance matrix and implementation mapping

This document preserves 44 independent synthetic semantic cases for the [generic constraint semantics](constraint-semantics.md), and maps them to the first implementation as of 2026-10-19. Their baseline counterparts are executable in [test_constraints_engine.py](../../tests/doe/test_constraints_engine.py). The prose expectations are broader than those bounded fixtures: a baseline counterpart does not establish the reduction half, complete source interpretation, or every fact named in a compound example. The [DOE goals and design principles](SkillFlow-DOE目标与设计原则.md) remain the governing baseline.

The case tables retain semantic expectations. The implementation mapping below records the actual status vocabulary and separate preservation coverage; [constraint-implementation.md](constraint-implementation.md) records interfaces, measured material sizes, validation runs, and unsupported behavior. No historical experiment is reclassified by these synthetic cases.

## Reading the expectations

- **Baseline check** concerns the original, frozen program model and the restriction taken from the sealed Task. A conclusion supported by that model is not an observation of execution or a proof that the source-to-model translation is complete.
- **Reduction check** concerns a proposed, boundary-local `exclude_field` candidate. A proposal is not presumed eligible. For every positive reduction expectation, the ordinary DOE prerequisites must be established independently: declared sensitivity, the relevant exposure relation, applicable positive preservation demands, and all other field-reduction eligibility conditions. Negative cases may fail those prerequisites as well as restriction preservation. A restriction never supplies the prerequisites by itself.
- A positive reduction expectation means that the restriction-specific preservation argument succeeds under the stated facts. It does not certify the entire task, the global minimum payload, or absence of DOE. Required positive information, other restrictions, earlier observations, and unresolved limitations remain independently relevant.
- An abstract bad path is not automatically a feasible counterexample. Where conditions or candidate correlations are unresolved, preserve that uncertainty. A guarantee over all paths of a sound over-approximation may support a model-relative conclusion; the presence of a bad path in that over-approximation alone does not establish a feasible violation.
- No case requires executing a reduced Skill, assigning necessity to every node, changing the sealed Task to fit current behavior, or modifying frozen propagation facts.
- Every rehearsal or bench session below denotes one task invocation unless a case explicitly uses an existing single-level `for_each` member scope. These examples introduce no arbitrary grouping keys, same-task session-reset mechanism, or cross-task counter.

## Execution condition

|---|---|---|---|

A private operator inscription is an unrelated payload field. | Establish the event-implies-condition property within the declared model, including the particular Boolean version. | Excluding the inscription preserves the restriction when the verified dependency closure excludes it from the condition, event occurrence, scope, and recipient identity. |

## Scoped occurrence bound

|---|---|---|---|
| CA-OC-A | A miniature railway simulation permits at most one gate pulse per rehearsal. A Boolean branch selects exactly one of two pulse sites. A count guarantee alone does not create a payload reduction. |

| CA-OC-C | The Task permits at most two successful gate pulses. ## Executable baseline mapping

`test_design_matrix_baseline` preserves all 44 case IDs. `potential_violation` intentionally covers both a direct static violation candidate and a bad abstract behavior; reasons distinguish them, and neither is reported as an observed execution. `not_established` retains missing semantics, incomplete scope, or an incomplete check. `rejected` below is a schema-validation test outcome, not a persisted constraint-check status.

| Actual baseline outcome | Case IDs |
|---|---|
| `satisfied` | CA-IP-E, CA-IP-F, CA-IP-G; CA-EC-A, CA-EC-C, CA-EC-E; CA-OC-A, CA-OC-D, CA-OC-F, CA-OC-G; CA-OR-A, CA-OR-E, CA-OR-F; CA-X-A, CA-X-J, CA-X-L |
| `potential_violation` | CA-IP-A, CA-IP-B, CA-IP-C, CA-IP-D, CA-IP-H, CA-IP-I; CA-EC-B, CA-EC-F, CA-EC-G; CA-OC-B, CA-OC-H, CA-OC-I; CA-OR-B, CA-OR-C, CA-OR-D; CA-X-I, CA-X-K |

| Schema rejected | CA-IP-J, CA-X-F |

The scope of those counterparts is deliberately bounded:

- CA-OC-B supplies three finite event occurrences. It tests occurrence counting, not inference of a mutable loop's exact trip count.
- CA-X-B exercises an incomplete coverage marker. Detecting a source requirement that an annotation silently narrows to selected sites still requires source/Task/declaration review; a valid selector alone cannot prove source completeness.
- CA-OR-E tests an invocation-local order monitor. The fixture does not orchestrate two task runs or add a same-task reset mechanism.
- CA-OR-F tests vacuity of precedence. Its independent positive action obligation belongs to demand coverage and review, not this baseline assertion.
- CA-X-J's baseline counterpart tests the original count with a false retry edge. Request-to-response change and downstream control are separate preservation obligations; passing this baseline test cannot discharge them.
- CA-X-K's baseline counterpart retains the information violation. The independent positive-demand gate is responsible for blocking deletion that conflicts with the demanded field.
- CA-IP-J rejects an invented stronger relationship field, and CA-X-F rejects nested scope syntax. Other unsupported expressions, unknown multiplicity, repeated-call matching, and incomplete facts can be well-formed inputs that yield `not_established`.

Additional baseline regressions cover condition-version availability, unknown or repeated request pairing, member scope revisits, task-wide member ordering, nested content paths, partial-order arrangements, empty member counts, and finite resource limits. They remain bounded model tests.

Real-pipeline information regressions additionally name an explicitly selected child as the prohibited source: its original parent whole and an earlier whole-model observation still contain that child, whereas explicit exclusion and independent overwrite remove the original value. Boolean regressions distinguish an opaque unknown field from a definitely absent field or invalid access; the latter remain `not_established`, including inside a syntactic tautology.

Recursive operand regressions cover missing fields within comparisons, scalar/object counts inside tautologies, Boolean field access through a normalized producer, transparent container types, and mixed known/opaque alternatives. Presence distinguishes missing from existing null, false and zero. Known invalid operands stay unsupported; unknown alternatives are not erased because another candidate is known true. These checks preserve the supported expression fragment rather than adding a general evaluator.

## Preservation coverage and remaining limits

[test_constraint_preservation.py](../../tests/doe/test_constraint_preservation.py) separately exercises boundary-local change propagation through model observation, selection, exclusion, overwrite, opaque processing, request/response links, public outputs, and saved reads with weak aliases or partial order. It also checks receiver separation for equal Data, retention of a baseline violation after an independent candidate is accepted, structural count preservation without positive seeds, deterministic closure, and rejection of counterfeit checks or preservation evidence by the shared result validator.

Its symbolic member regressions distinguish overlap from full coverage: an `each` change reaches a selected fixed member, while excluding or overwriting one fixed member does not erase change to the whole member range. These tests preserve the same candidate eligibility boundary.

[test_constraint_scope_influence.py](../../tests/doe/test_constraint_scope_influence.py) tests the critical empty-member counterexample: the baseline can satisfy a constraint because a collection is empty, while changing the collection can create member occurrences. The candidate is retained as `not_established`; existing zero instances and baseline satisfaction cannot prove preservation.

These tests do not assert that all positive reduction expectations in the 44-case table are supported. In particular, changed collection membership or identity, task-wide ordering across members, and request correspondence requiring fresh identities across repeated CFG invocations remain outside the supported preservation boundary. An unpaired opaque tool/remote delivery is not certified terminal merely because its response record is absent. A complete supported terminal delivery can use the subset rule; other complete disjoint closures can use no-influence, with every other DOE gate still active.

The implementation reference records the executed test batches without summing overlapping runs. Those runs demonstrate their tested rules only; they do not prove complete natural-language interpretation, unrestricted path feasibility, or globally minimal sensitive-data use.
